## 2. The stop conditions

Six conditions plus the unchecked rule. Each one names the class it belongs to, because a
risk bound and an inference are different things and conflating them is how you either ignore a
real loss or kill a sleeve on noise.

| id | class | what trips it | what to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S6** | CRITICAL | the workers' --tags do not equal the armed five, on either account | stop and read the launch command before believing anything else on the page |
| **S1a** | RISK_BOUND_not_inference | cumulative NET R since arming falls below the floor the owner pre-registered | remove the sleeve from --tags on that account (orchestrator ceremony) |
| **S1b** | INFERENCE | cumulative NET R falls below the level at which a trip is INFORMATIVE | a trip here is evidence the sleeve is not running at its archive expectancy. It is still not a significance test — read it with the day-blocked p S2 publishes. |
| **S2** | RISK_BOUND_not_inference | cumulative GROSS R (before any cost) negative on BOTH the trade-weighted and the day-weighted mean, over >= 20 fills spanning >= 8 day blocks | remove from --tags; a sleeve losing before cost cannot be rescued by a better cost model |
| **S3** | ECONOMIC | mean swap nights per closed position exceeds the sleeve's measured p90 (alert) or its break-even (stop) | alert: read it. stop: remove from --tags — the tier restatement is void. |
| **S4** | CONTRACT_FIDELITY | median hold since arming exceeds 3x the sleeve's archive median | alert. The published economics describe a different contract; re-price before acting on them. |
| **S5** | RISK_BOUND_not_inference | 9 consecutive closes at the stop on one sleeve on one account | alert at 6: look. stop at 9: remove from --tags. |
| **S7** | UNCHECKED | any condition above could not be evaluated (no fills file, no export, no basis) | exit code 3, never 0. 'nothing is wrong' and 'nothing was checked' are different, and collapsing them is how .tools/monitor_books.py sat mute through the window it was meant to be watching. |

### S1 — TWO floors, because one number cannot answer two questions

This page had **one** floor in its first cut, `-max(3.0, 12 × |archive net expectancy|)`,
justified by a claim about its false-alarm rate that had never been computed. Computing it
**refuted the claim**, and the refutation is worth more than the original number:

| account · sleeve | archive net R/trade | **risk floor** | how often it fires on a HEALTHY sleeve | **evidence floor** | its false-alarm rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| FTMO · `crypto` | +1.03128 | -12.38 R | 0.0% | -12.38 R | 0.0% |
| FTMO · `energy_agri` | +0.43215 | -5.19 R | **35%** | -11.19 R | 10.5% |
| FTMO · `sub_xvol_pullback` | +1.24597 | -14.95 R | 0.0% | -14.95 R | 0.0% |
| FTMO · `fx_jpy` | +0.04101 | -3.00 R | **74%** | -18.00 R | 9.2% |
| FTMO · `sub_mid_dn_revert` | +0.23939 | -3.00 R | **58%** | -13.50 R | 9.8% |
| redacted_account · `crypto` | +1.08734 | -13.05 R | 0.0% | -13.05 R | 0.0% |
| redacted_account · `energy_agri` | +0.40605 | -4.87 R | **38%** | -12.37 R | 9.6% |
| redacted_account · `sub_xvol_pullback` | +1.22592 | -14.71 R | 0.0% | -14.71 R | 0.0% |
| redacted_account · `fx_jpy` | +0.01905 | -3.00 R | **77%** | -18.50 R | 10.0% |
| redacted_account · `sub_mid_dn_revert` | +0.23015 | -3.00 R | **58%** | -13.50 R | 10.0% |

**Read the third column.** On `fx_jpy` the −3.00 R floor fires **74–77 % of the time on a
sleeve running at exactly its own archive expectancy.** On `sub_mid_dn_revert` it is 58 %, on
`energy_agri` 35–38 %. Those are not tripwires; they are coin flips wearing a threshold's
clothes — and I only found that out by computing the number I had already claimed.

**Why, and it is structural rather than a bad choice of constant.** `fx_jpy`'s expectancy is
+0.04101 R/trade against a
per-trade dispersion of 1.611 R. A
near-zero-drift random walk with that step size passes −3 R almost surely. **There is no
threshold on that sleeve that is both tight and sound**, and pretending otherwise would have
been the worst thing on this page.

So both floors are published and each answers its own question:

- **the risk floor** is *what you pre-registered as the price of finding out*. It is a
  statement of your tolerance and it is a perfectly good one. When it fires, the tool now says
  on the same line how often it fires on a healthy sleeve, so nobody reads it as evidence.
- **the evidence floor** is *the depth at which a trip means something*: the deepest 0.5 R step
  whose false-alarm rate is ≤ 10%
  over 60 fills, by bootstrap over each
  sleeve's **own** centred archive R distribution — measured, not assumed normal, because these
  distributions have a stop-out mass at −1 R and a long right tail and a normal approximation
  understates the left tail exactly where this question lives.

On `crypto` and `sub_xvol_pullback` the two coincide: their expectancy dwarfs their dispersion,
so the risk floor was already safe. **The gap only opens on the sleeves that were armed without
a passed gate**, which is not a coincidence.

### S2 — negative GROSS, and the repair the live record forced

A sleeve losing before any cost is charged cannot be rescued by a better cost model. That is
what the two dead JPY sleeves looked like and it took a fortnight to see. But the tripwire now
requires the loss on **both** weightings, and here is why:

> `fx_jpy`'s 21 FTMO fills read **-0.2487 R/fill**
> trade-weighted and **-0.1450** day-weighted — a
> 42%
> attenuation on the same fills, because the losing days carry up to four fills and the winning
> days carry one. The decision day is the dependence unit (Session R measured lag-1 rho 0.511),
> so a trade-weighted-only tripwire reports a sleeve's *worst* reading as its verdict.

It also computes both weightings **per account**. Pooling the two accounts into one day block
mixes two independent books and flips the pooled day-weighted figure positive (+0.0563), which is
an artifact of the pooling and not a property of either account. This page had that wrong in a
first cut and the per-account split is what corrected it.

## 4. One command

```bash
python3 scripts/book_sleeve_telemetry.py \
    --fills <dir>/LIVE_TRADE_ROWS.jsonl \
    --armed-utc 2026-07-30T11:52:00Z \
    --launch-tags-ftmo crypto,energy_agri,sub_xvol_pullback,fx_jpy,sub_mid_dn_revert \
    --launch-tags-redacted_account crypto,energy_agri,sub_xvol_pullback,fx_jpy,sub_mid_dn_revert
```

Everything is optional except that without `--fills` nothing economic evaluates. **There is no
live feed.** The file is built from a read-only VPS export:

```bash
# 1. export the VPS's MT5 API state (read-only; the orchestrator's ceremony)
#    -> <export>/09_mt5_api/{ftmo,redacted_account}_history_{deals,orders}_get.jsonl
# 2. turn it into priced trade rows
python3 scripts/w7_live_forensics.py --export-root <export> --out-dir <dir>
#    -> <dir>/LIVE_TRADE_ROWS.jsonl        <- the --fills file
```

So the economic conditions are only as fresh as the last export. **Until one exists with
post-arming fills in it, the page says so and exits 3, not 0** — 'nothing is wrong' and 'nothing
was checked' are different, and collapsing them is how `.tools/monitor_books.py` sat mute through
the window it was meant to be watching.

Exit codes: `0` clean · `1` a STOP or an ALERT · `3` clean, but something could not be checked.

Run it **when you look**, not on a timer. Expect ~7 book-days a month; long silences are normal.

This page complements `phase4/CANARY_OPERATOR_PAGE.md` rather than replacing it: that one answers
*is it armed, is it alive, what is the headroom, is the cost model still right*. This one answers
*what is each of the five sleeves actually doing, and has any of them reached the line*.

## 5. If you decide to take a sleeve back off

**How.** remove the sleeve from `--tags` in scripts/run_book_supervisor.ps1 on the host and restart the supervisor (the orchestrator's ceremony)

**What it stops.** NEW generation only — book_engine.py:493 applies tags

**What it does NOT stop — and this is the half that matters.** exit management of an already-open position. `_manageable_pairs` (book_owner.py:2694) calls active_specs(None, ...) with tags NOT applied, and its consumers are manage_open_positions (:1985) and _alert_out_of_universe (:2093) — so a de-tagged sleeve's open position stays adopted and exit-managed. This is the SAFE direction and is why de-arming does not need a flatten.

**The same-day size consequence.** narrowing --tags mid-day does NOT reduce that day's Kelly-lite conviction count: RunningConvictionLedger.update_and_count unions the day's firing sleeves and admission.py:1210 takes na = max(na, override). The other four sleeves keep the 5-sleeve multiplier until the decision day rolls. De-arm at a day boundary if the size step matters.

**Never.** do NOT reach for live_broker_authority: false — H8. At HEAD the flatten suppression is book_owner.py:2382 (the marker row; the return follows) and the observe-only degradation is :2544. Flatten first, confirm flat, THEN shut a gate. (CLAUDE.md's :2364-2373 / :2526 are one commit stale; the behaviour is unchanged.)

Two `--tags` failure modes to know before the ceremony, both already measured: `--tags ""` is
falsy at `run_book.py:340` and silently means **all BUILT sleeves** — fail-open; and an all-typo
`--tags` yields an empty spec list and the book stands down silently every tick
(`registry.py:144`) — fail-closed, but mute. That is why S6 is the first check on the page and
why nothing below it means anything if it is red.
