## Three things this ordering says out loud

**1. The cheapest item on the map is a precondition for the next activation.** F15 — a
read-only `copy_rates` probe on the two terminals, 0.2 MH — decides whether `mx_btcusd` can
be armed at all. After AQ's time-stop repair an open `mx_*` position asks for 7,744 M15 bars
a tick, and a short feed makes the time stop **inert** rather than late. Arming before that
probe is arming a sleeve whose backstop may not exist.

**2. The parked campaign costs 72× the item that would move an armed sleeve.** F13 is
36 MH for a verdict the evidence already points at (pooled must exceed +0.1 from −0.152;
four negative January arms), and it cannot legally run until a pooled evaluator that **does
not exist anywhere** has been written and sealed. F1 is 0.5 MH and removes a by-hand
substitution three sessions have had to remember about an **armed** sleeve. Putting both on
one page is the point of the page.

**3. The highest-value walkforward item is retrospective.** F6 re-gates published receipts
at each sleeve's live exit contract. AR's own winner lost 58 % of its measured advantage
that way, and AQ's Side-B table already found ten sleeves whose published economics describe
a contract the book does not run. This is the only item on the map that can change a number
already in front of the owner.
