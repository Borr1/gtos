## 7. F31 exactly, and why it does not disturb B7.5 (B33/B34)

**What F31 is** [VERIFIED, G1A:147-241 / IS:819-871]. The replay's exit simulator (`src/research/dynamic_execution_policy.py`) books R two ways: **level** exits — stop (`:428`), giveback (`:509`), final target (`:462-467`) — take a *threshold* and consult no price; **mark** exits — path end (`:641`), time stop (`:626`) — book the observation's own price-derived R. In tick mode each observation is a single executable close-side quote with `open_r = high_r = low_r = close_r` (`v4_timewarp…:60797-60810`). So a level exit fires *because* the observed R has already crossed the threshold — and then books the **threshold**, discarding the crossing quote's own (worse) R. The model therefore grants **exactly zero gap-through/adverse close-side slippage**. Measured over the 212 covered level rows across the four sealed January arms: stops **107/107 adverse**, Σ −4.901 R (every stop books exactly −1.0); giveback **97/97 adverse**, Σ −3.733 R (the identity `final_r == mfe_r − giveback_close_r` holds exactly on 121/121); final targets **0/8 adverse**, +0.539 R — conservative and correct, a resting limit legitimately fills at its level, so the defect is specific to stops and giveback. Net **−8.095 R**, mean −0.038 R/trade — and it is a **lower bound**: 47 level-family rows had no executable close quote and were refused, never imputed, and they are the `stop_reached_before_target`/`target_reached_before_stop` families — the most gap-through-prone population is the least measured (an imputing verifier gets −10.169 R, cited as corroboration only). It is **uncompensated**: the flat `expected_slippage_r = 0.02` (`agent_config.yaml:740`) totals 6.50 R over all 325 physical rows — 80.3 % of the lower bound — and measured adverse *entry* slippage is zero (entry drift is +2.93..+3.08 R favourable per arm, already banked), so the allowance is not consumed elsewhere. The engine **declares the boundary** (`close_side_all_in_cost_status: "not_joined_in_replay_net_proxy_r"`) but nothing claims the 0.02 discharges it, and the experiment protocol contains **zero occurrences of the string "slippage"** — "stated boundary, uncompensated" is the receipt's own precise phrasing.

**Why it does not disturb B7.5** [VERIFIED, B34 / G1A §4a]. B7.5's decision object is the **arm contrast** (selection vs sizing decomposition), not absolute economics, and the defect is close to common-mode: all four arms run the same exit model, and the per-arm gap is nearly uniform (−1.99 / −2.12 / −1.95 / −2.04 R). Restating every covered level exit at its price-derived R — netting each trade's own 0.02 so the allowance already charged is not charged twice, with the un-netted figure published as an upper bound because what the 0.02 covers is undocumented: **arm ordering unchanged** (S0R0 > S1R0 > S0R1 > S1R1, both bases); **all four factorial classifications unchanged**; the largest shift is **0.0135 against the protocol's ±0.1 materiality band** — an order of magnitude inside; and the per-trade gap (0.029–0.035 R) already sits inside the campaign's own `extra_cost_0.05r_per_trade` stress band whose classifications are unchanged at 0.05. Hence **no re-run indicated and April is not blocked** (B36 adds that the April partial could not carry a conclusion anyway: 25.8 % coverage, 3 level exits). What does move is **absolute** economics — the reference arm's q goes −0.0224 → −0.0316 netted (−0.0444 upper bound), i.e. it loses ~1.4–2.0× per risk dollar what the seal states — which binds at exactly one place: the canary-readiness decision, where absolute economics is the criterion rather than an arm contrast. That is why the receipt routes the repair (a gap-through term) to the Phase-2 core rather than to a campaign halt.
