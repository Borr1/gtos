## Final verdict (1 paragraph)

Alpha's top-5 findings partially hold up but two of them have methodological weight smaller than the written ranking implies. **The XAUUSD entry-quality degradation (Rank #1) is the most defensible finding in the report** and triangulates independently with ε's "fast-loss" (26.9% → 69.2%, Fisher p=0.017) and ε's MFE compression (0.417R → 0.106R), and is directionally consistent with γ's observation that XAUUSD L2 rejections are correctly edge-negative throughout. **But it sits inside an inferential problem δ flagged separately**: δ's bootstrap shows XAUUSD quarterly WR spread is within noise-band (p=0.7525), and δ warns the "2025Q3 pivot" is post-hoc selection. So "entry-quality degradation" may be a regime-ADR ×5 artifact, not an entry-placement failure. **Variant C (Rank #2) is weaker than alpha presents**: the claimed +0.128R/trade shrinks to +0.007R/trade (meanR delta) when a defensible assumption (remaining 67% runs to the same exit as actual, not to BE) is used; paired-t collapses to p=0.92. **NAS100 "deep MFE then reversal" (Rank #3) reproduces directionally but the exact per-loss numbers in §6 could not be replicated from the scratch script** — the scratch reads `mfe_r=0.0` on every NAS100 loss (T7 sim doesn't populate this field for NAS100), and my CSV-replay reconstruction produces a different vector (median 2.33R vs alpha's 1.66R) with the same qualitative shape. **The single most important correction the chairman should apply**: downgrade Variant C from "medium evidence at +0.128R/trade" to "shadow-logger-assumption-dependent, between +0.007R/trade (defensible) and +0.128R/trade (BE-on-partial assumption). Needs explicit CEO decision on partial+BE coupling before the R-impact can be scored."

## Concerns ranked by severity

1. **[MAJOR] Variant C "+0.128R/trade" masks a load-bearing accounting assumption** — `shadow_logs/partial_close_backtest.jsonl` record schema sets `remaining_r: 0.0` on every LOSS (verified: all 9 reversed_past_entry losses show `remaining_r=0.0, variant_c_blended_r=0.33`). This bakes in "when partial fills, move the remaining to BE". If instead remaining 67% runs to actual exit (no BE coupling), blended-R per loss = 0.33×1.0 + 0.67×(−1.0) = **−0.34R**, not +0.33R. Under that alternative: n=45 mean delta **+0.007R/trade**, paired-t **p=0.9158**, Wilcoxon **p=0.3692** — effectively zero. Alpha's own prose on `alpha_entry_execution.md:202` acknowledges the "-0.34R" framing but still reports the aggregate as +0.128R without flagging the coupling assumption. **Recommended action:** chairman must state the assumption explicitly and split findings by assumption regime. This single assumption moves "Variant C" from "medium actionable" to "shadow-logger artifact" in severity class.

2. **[MAJOR] NAS100 loss-MFE vector [0, 0.57, 1.82, 1.71, 1.66, 1.62, 5.60, 3.90, 1.97, 0.94, 1.39] (`alpha_entry_execution.md:284`) does not reproduce from `_alpha_scratch/slippage_analysis.py`** — the scratch script reads T7 sim `mfe_r` which is 0.0 for all 11 NAS100 losses (I verified `Counter('mfe_r' == 0.0) == 11`). My own M15 CSV-replay reconstruction with no window cap produces `[0, 0.57, 2.33, 2.95, 3.18, 2.69, 5.60, 3.90, 1.97, 0.94, 1.39]` (median 2.33R, mean 2.32R). Four of 11 values differ meaningfully from alpha's (the Feb-26/Mar-4/Mar-13/Mar-17 entries: my values 2.33, 2.95, 3.18, 2.69 vs alpha 1.82, 1.71, 1.66, 1.62). With 16-candle (4h) window cap, I get `[0, 0.57, 2.03, 2.49, 3.18, 1.92, 5.60, 3.90, 1.97, 0.94, 1.39]` (still doesn't match alpha exactly). **Recommended action:** either (a) add a separate reconstruction script to `_alpha_scratch/` showing exactly how the vector was derived, or (b) have chairman accept only the qualitative directional finding (9/11 had mfe≥1R, etc. — which does reproduce) and drop the point-estimate median=1.66R number from any quantitative claim.

3. **[MAJOR] BE-stop counterfactual math (`alpha_entry_execution.md:150-155`) uses an unrealistic "win@1.5R" ceiling** — the CF formula `would_have_won × 1.5 + (not_won) × (−1.0)` assumes every BE-stop-proxy trade, if allowed to run, would have closed at +1.5R. Real XAUUSD win R distribution (verified in batch KB, n=79 wins): **median 0.490R, mean 0.859R**, with 60 of 79 wins (76%) closing BELOW 1R. The naive CF overstates the "BE saves 13R" claim because the imagined alternative outcome (+1.5R) is in the 94th percentile of actual wins. **Recommended action:** either (a) recompute CF using actual win-R empirical distribution, or (b) strike section 3 BE-trigger R-impact numbers and recast only the directional "partial_close shadow shows +2.97R locked vs −8.11R actual on 9 rescues" — which does reproduce.

4. **[MAJOR] Pre-vs-post 2025Q3 cutoff is post-hoc selection and inflates the "doubled" framing** — alpha reports 26.3% → 50.0% bad-entry rate using a 2025-07-01 cutoff. But `knowledge_base_backtest` has only n=19 pre-losses and n=20 post-losses at that cutoff (or n=21 if loss defined by r<0 instead of outcome=='LOSS'). Two-prop z p=0.1286 (alpha). Fisher's exact p=0.1908 (my calc; Fisher is the conservative choice at n<30). Cohen's h = 0.493 (medium effect). **95% CI for difference in proportions spans 0: [−0.058, +0.532].** δ's bootstrap says the quarterly WR ordering across the full series is not distinguishable from random partitioning (p=0.7525). Taken together: the "doubling" is **directional evidence only**, hinges on where the cut was drawn, and has effect-size overlapping zero. Alpha does tag it "directional, not Bonferroni-safe" — good — but then ranks it #1 with "estimated R impact −2 to −4R/quarter", which reads as much stronger than the CI supports.

5. **[MAJOR] Alpha's XAUUSD entry-degradation claim conflicts with δ's regime-decay null** — δ (section 1, `alpha_entry_execution.md`'s TL;DR #1 says decay is noise-band, bootstrap p=0.7525). δ section 1 table: 2026-Q1 ADR = 169.7 vs 2025-Q1 ADR = 36.4 (**4.7× vol expansion**), ATR/252d = 2.16 (highest in series), KER (end-of-quarter) collapsed to 0.033 (5× choppier than Q4). If the 2026Q1 "bad-entry" rate explosion to 61.5% is confounded with a 4.7× ADR expansion and a KER collapse, then the "entry quality degraded" hypothesis is underdetermined vs a "noise regime tripled" hypothesis. Alpha does note "This is consistent with AI is placing limit entries too close to tops of ranges..." but doesn't address the ADR-driven alternative. **Triangulation with ε is partial** (ε's fast-loss pattern is XAUUSD-specific, which is useful), but ε itself does not survive strict Bonferroni (α=0.0083 per ε's own math), only BH FDR. **Recommended action:** chairman must present entry-degradation as a two-hypothesis claim: (A) AI degradation, (B) XAUUSD regime-ADR shift. CEO decision to prioritize prompt-level fix vs regime-conditioning fix depends on which.

6. **[MINOR] Sign test chosen where Wilcoxon signed-rank is the appropriate non-parametric pair** — alpha reports sign test p=0.2327 on n=45 paired delta samples (`alpha_entry_execution.md:188`). Wilcoxon signed-rank is uniformly more powerful when magnitudes matter. My re-run: **Wilcoxon p=0.3692** (still not significant). Paired t p=0.1987. Switching to the more appropriate test actually makes the Variant C case directionally WEAKER, not stronger. Alpha's sign-test framing is honest about n but understates the uncertainty. **Recommended action:** chairman cite Wilcoxon p=0.369 as the headline, not sign-test p=0.23.

7. **[MINOR] "Bad entry" definition (mfe < 0.2R) is a single hand-picked threshold** — alpha never tests robustness to 0.1R, 0.15R, 0.25R, 0.3R. With a single threshold the result could reflect outcome labeling rather than a true pattern. Verified alpha's script uses `t['mfe_r'] < 0.2` at `slippage_analysis.py:378`. **Recommended action:** add robustness table (0.1R, 0.2R, 0.3R bad-entry thresholds) before chairman relies on the 61.5% 2026Q1 figure. I did not re-run this but a quick sensitivity check would be low-cost.

8. **[MINOR] 2026Q2 sample (n=2 losses in Section 2 quarterly evolution) is too small to carry any signal but appears in the Q-over-Q table** — `alpha_entry_execution.md:108` shows Q2-2026 with n=2. Alpha does note "2026Q2 is only 2 weeks of data" but the row still appears next to Q1 n=14, inviting misreading. Would prefer suppression or strong caveat.

9. **[MINOR] Slippage / "becoming liquidity" null finding is presented as "0/16 = hypothesis not supported"** — this is fine qualitatively. At n=16 with a 3-conjunction signature (bit-exact + reversed + 1R threshold), the power to detect a 20% signature rate is low. A Hanley-McNeil-style 95% upper CI on a 0/16 binomial is 0 to ~17.1%. Alpha correctly labels this "NULL, directional, exploratory" on line 365. But the section-header phrasing "liquidity-hunt thesis NOT supported" overshoots what n=16 can say. **Recommended action:** chairman language: "No signature detected in n=16 sample; 95% upper CI ≤ 17% signature rate; would need n≥100 for a confident null." This is a writing-style issue, not a numerical error.

10. **[MINOR] Reproducibility script runs clean and produces every other headline number** — the scratch is self-contained, no external dependencies, no secret inputs. All "Section 4 Variant C" and "Section 5 Quarterly Evolution" and "Section 2 bit-exact signature" numbers reproduce bit-exact. This is a clear strength.

## Cross-agent conflict / triangulation notes

- **α vs δ (PARTIAL CONFLICT):** α's top finding is XAUUSD entry-quality degradation. δ's top finding is "decay is noise-band" (bootstrap p=0.7525) and "the observed WR decay does not reproduce bit-exact; the canonical 73.2→71.4→63.6→59.4 is post-hoc selection". δ's framing dominates at the aggregate WR level; α's framing is specifically about entry quality (mfe<0.2R on losses). These are NOT the same metric — α could be right at the entry-geometry layer while δ is right at the trade-outcome layer. But α's R-impact estimate (−2 to −4R/quarter) implicitly treats entry-quality degradation as a WR-impacting leak, which δ says the data does not distinguish from noise. **Chairman should carve a careful path**: "entry geometry has degraded directionally (α); quarterly WR has NOT (δ); therefore the entry-geometry degradation is either (i) not yet biting in aggregate, or (ii) offset by other improvements." Both are consistent with the evidence.

- **α vs ε (STRONG TRIANGULATION):** α reports XAUUSD bad-entry (mfe<0.2R) rate 26% → 50%. ε reports XAUUSD fast-loss (mfe<0.3R) rate 26.9% → 69.2% (Fisher p=0.017). These are different thresholds on the same underlying metric and directions agree. ε explicitly performs BH FDR correction across 6 signatures and the XAUUSD fast-loss SURVIVES at rank 2 (threshold 0.0167, p=0.017). ε also finds XAUUSD winner-MFE compressed 1.04R → 0.49R (Mann-Whitney p=0.020, passes BH at rank 3). **This is the strongest quantitative finding in the Phase 1 corpus** and α's Rank #1 should be upgraded from "directional, p=0.13" to "triangulated by ε with BH FDR significance at XAUUSD-specific". One caveat: ε itself flags that XAUUSD-specific absence in other instruments is consistent with either "XAUUSD algo-arb" OR "XAUUSD regime shift" — the mechanism is not resolved.

- **α vs γ (PARTIAL TRIANGULATION on NAS100):** α ranks "NAS100 deep MFE then reversal" as a leak. γ says NAS100 has a genuine mid-March regime shift (BL decay Jan-93% → Apr-40%, Fisher p=2×10⁻⁵ Bonferroni-safe). If NAS100 regime shifted mid-March, then α's 11 NAS100 losses straddle the regime-shift boundary — pooled analysis may mix two different processes. γ's framing makes a stronger case than α's for **treating pre-Mar-15 and post-Mar-15 NAS100 separately**. α does not split.

- **α vs ζ (TANGENTIAL):** ζ finds material bugs in `market_state.py` detector (`identify_structure` all-history HH/HL, OB wick-vs-body mitigation inconsistency, `_count_touches` off-by-one). Chairman should weight ζ's code-bug findings BEFORE α's AI-prompt-level recommendation.
