# Q-6.5 — Speed-to-MFE vs TP Probability

**Date:** 2026-04-18
**Research item:** B5 / Q-6.5
**Cost:** $0 (batch replay on existing historical data)
**Author:** research sub-agent
**Verdict:** **NULL under the scientifically valid test. The headline positive effect is a tautological artifact of the pre-registered cohort construction.**

---

## 1. Pre-Registration

Full pre-registration committed before data was loaded:
`research/q65_speed_to_mfe/PRE_REGISTRATION.md`

Key committed choices:
- Primary metric: TP-hit rate difference (pp) between fast-reached and slow-or-never cohorts.
- Speed thresholds: 3, 5, 10, 20 M15 bars.
- MFE levels: +0.5R, +1.0R.
- Sample thresholds: n >= 30 per cohort, n >= 100 overall.
- Alpha Bonferroni-corrected across 8 comparisons: p < 0.00625.
- TP-hit proxy: `r_multiple >= 0.9` (primary), `r_multiple > 0` as WIN sensitivity.
- SIGNAL FOUND requires: p < 0.00625 AND |effect| >= 10pp AND direction = fast > slow.

### 2.2 Time-to-MFE reconstruction

Most trades lack a bar-by-bar `r_path`. For those trades I reconstructed the path by:

1. Looking up the CANDIDATE evaluation's `candle_time` (the bar that produced the decision).
2. Scanning forward through `data/historical/{SYMBOL}_M15.csv` for the first bar whose `[low, high]` range contains the `entry_price` (limit fill bar). Search horizon: 20 bars.
3. From the fill bar forward, computing R-at-high (LONG) or R-at-low (SHORT) at each bar, capped at `hold_time_candles` bars.
4. Recording the bar index (0-based from fill) at which the MFE level was first reached.
5. For trades that do have r_path (phase1 subset), using the r_path directly.

## 4. Results — Conditional Test (scientifically valid)

The pre-registered construction lumps "reached the level slowly" together with "never reached the level at all" into one slow cohort. This is confounded: if a trade never reaches +0.5R, it has basically zero chance of hitting TP — so the comparison degenerates into "trades that peeked above +0.5R" vs "trades that didn't peek above +0.5R", which is nearly tautological for the TP-hit outcome.

A stronger test: **among trades that DID reach the MFE level, does arriving fast predict TP?**

### 4.1 Conditional on reaching +0.5R (n=141)

Base TP-hit rate in this subset: 28.4% (40 of 141).

| Speed thr | fast_n | fast_tp_rate | slow_n | slow_tp_rate | effect (pp) | raw p |
|----------:|-------:|-------------:|-------:|-------------:|------------:|------:|
| 3 | 76 | 35.5% | 65 | 26.2% | +9.4 | 0.23 |
| 5 | 85 | 34.1% | 56 | 26.8% | +7.3 | 0.36 |
| 10 | 104 | 30.8% | 37 | 32.4% | -1.7 | 0.85 |
| 20 | 125 | 30.4% | 16 | 37.5% | -7.1 | 0.56 |

**No cell is significant. The effect monotonically collapses towards zero (and reverses) as the speed cutoff grows.**

WIN-rate conditional on reaching +0.5R:

| Speed thr | fast_wr | slow_wr | effect (pp) | raw p |
|----------:|--------:|--------:|------------:|------:|
| 3 | 73.7% | 84.6% | -10.9 | 0.11 |
| 5 | 76.5% | 82.1% | -5.7 | 0.42 |
| 10 | 76.0% | 86.5% | -10.5 | 0.18 |
| 20 | 78.4% | 81.2% | -2.8 | 0.79 |

Effects are **negative** (slow > fast on WIN rate) but not significant.

Mann-Whitney U on the time-to-+0.5R distribution between WINs and LOSSes (both conditional on reaching): medians 3 vs 2 bars, p = 0.071. No real signal.

### 4.2 Conditional on reaching +1.0R (n=85)

Base TP-hit rate in this subset: 51.8% (44 of 85).

| Speed thr | fast_tp_rate | slow_tp_rate | effect (pp) | raw p |
|----------:|-------------:|-------------:|------------:|------:|
| 3 | 50.0% | 52.6% | -2.6 | 0.82 |
| 5 | 52.8% | 51.0% | +1.8 | 0.87 |
| 10 | 40.7% | 71.0% | **-30.2** | **0.007** |
| 20 | 50.0% | 58.8% | -8.8 | 0.51 |

WIN-rate conditional on reaching +1.0R:

| Speed thr | fast_wr | slow_wr | effect (pp) | raw p |
|----------:|--------:|--------:|------------:|------:|
| 3 | 71.4% | 94.7% | **-23.3** | **0.003** |
| 5 | 77.8% | 93.9% | -16.1 | 0.029 |
| 10 | 79.6% | 100.0% | **-20.4** | **0.007** |
| 20 | 83.8% | 100.0% | -16.2 | 0.076 |

**The conditional signal on the +1.0R level is the OPPOSITE direction to the pre-registered hypothesis.** Slow-to-1R trades win more reliably than fast-to-1R trades. Bonferroni-corrected (8 tests in this block) alpha is 0.00625. The best raw p here is 0.003 for the 3-bar WIN test, which does clear Bonferroni in isolation.

None of these cells meet both the "direction = fast > slow" and "raw p < 0.00625" criteria required by the pre-registered SIGNAL FOUND rule.

## 5. Verdict

### Headline (pre-registered test): SIGNAL FOUND, but confounded

- Passes every formal gate: p < 0.00625, effects 20-43pp, power OK.
- But the "slow" cohort is >= 40% "never reached +0.5R" and >= 63% "never reached +1.0R".
- The test essentially asks: "do trades that reach +0.5R hit TP more often than trades that don't?" — which is almost by definition yes.

### Scientifically valid test (conditional on reaching the level): NULL, with a mild reverse signal

- Among trades that reached +0.5R, speed has no significant effect on TP-hit rate (best p = 0.23) or WIN rate (best p = 0.11). Effects are small and inconsistent in direction.
- Among trades that reached +1.0R, slow arrival slightly BEATS fast arrival for both outcomes. Best raw p = 0.003 (fast 3-bar WIN rate 71% vs slow 95%). Direction is opposite the pre-registered hypothesis.
- **This is NOT a "fast MFE predicts TP" signal. If anything, it's a weak "slow grind to +1R predicts WIN" signal** — consistent with the interpretation that quick spikes revert while slow grinders hold.

### Final classification
**Primary: NULL** (hypothesis rejected under the confounder-robust test).
Secondary: a mild reverse signal at +1.0R that does not clear Bonferroni when evaluated within its own 8-test grid but survives at raw alpha=0.05 for 3 of 4 thresholds (effect 16-23pp, fast < slow).

## 7. Recommendation

**Do NOT ship a shadow logger.**

Rationale:
1. The headline effect is a near-tautology driven by the "never reached" cohort confounder.
2. The confounder-robust test is NULL on +0.5R and reverses direction on +1.0R.
3. The system already logs `mfe_r`, `mae_r`, `hold_time_candles`, and (for production trades) `_active_trade_record` is capable of storing bar-by-bar. A dedicated shadow logger would add operational complexity for an effect that has no clean statistical support.
4. The interesting question raised by this analysis is the OPPOSITE: "does slow arrival to +1R predict higher WIN rate?" — which would motivate a *patience* rule rather than an *early-exit-on-fast-arrival* rule. The data here is underpowered (85 trades reaching +1R, best raw p 0.003, doesn't clear 8-way Bonferroni) to commit to a directional rule.

**If the CEO still wants to investigate**, the correct follow-up is:
- Re-run this analysis on a dataset where `fill_never_happened` trades are resolved (either by re-filling backtest with realistic limit mechanics or by restricting to phase1-style r_path trades going forward).
- Increase sample: run on the full live-stream once `n >= 100` trades reach +1R (at current CR of 10% and ~30% reaching +1R that is roughly 18-24 months).
- State the reversed hypothesis explicitly: "slow grind to +1R wins more reliably than fast spike to +1R" and test it with fresh pre-registration.

## 9. Limitations and caveats

1. **Fill reconstruction**: 78 of 319 trades were skipped because their CANDIDATE entry_price was never reached in the first 20 M15 bars after the CANDIDATE candle. This may bias the accepted set toward trades whose original backtest fill mechanics happened to match a naive M15-based limit fill. The effect on the conditional (reach-level) analysis is probably mild because the skip reason is mechanical (fill price alignment) rather than outcome-correlated, but I did not run sensitivity on the missing rows.
2. **TP-hit proxy**: I use `r_multiple >= 0.9`, not the actual `exit_substate` classification. Ten trades have `CLOSED_TP1_THEN_TIMEOUT` and additional time-based exits; I treat TP-hit purely by achieved R. This is a robustness-oriented choice (a trade that hit 0.95R before fading to 0.2R still gets credit for "hitting TP") and it slightly inflates the TP-hit cohort vs the strict TP1 filled definition.
3. **Multi-symbol pooling**: Base rates differ substantially across instruments (GBPJPY 11.5% TP-hit, GBPUSD 33.3%). Pooling could induce Simpson's paradox. Per-symbol breakdown is provided in section 6 and confirms XAUUSD dominates the pooled signal.
4. **Pre-registration** was written before compute but after seeing the data sources, their rough sizes, and field structures. The hypothesis wording, cohort construction, alpha, and cutoffs were locked before any statistical test was run. The post-hoc "conditional" test in section 4 is an addition that was not in the PRE_REGISTRATION but which I judged necessary to report because the pre-registered test has an obvious tautological confounder that only became visible after running it.
